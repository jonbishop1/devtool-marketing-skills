# Marketing plan playbook

## Fictional product context

TraceNest is a fictional local HTTP replay tool. An engineer can replay a sanitized fixture locally, but service integration needs a platform owner. Sample replay is not proof of recurring project value. Do not invent hosted behavior, speed improvements, or customer results.

## Decision to resolve

Sequence task-specific discovery, a runnable example, own-project success, and team adoption. If setup fails, prioritize evaluation reliability before additional distribution. Measure qualified projects and recurring useful tasks instead of stars or package downloads.

## Task-specific method

Pair discoverable technical content or ecosystem distribution with a working README or quickstart. Track qualified projects and repeated team use, not stars as a revenue proxy.

## Completed example

Five marketing hours/week; 6/10 selected support notes mention a missing local-service prerequisite; visits increasing. Working hypothesis: prerequisite discovery hurts evaluation. Week-one allocation: 2h draft a clear service prerequisite and proposed success check in a new evaluation asset; 1h engineering review; 1h observe an authorized sample attempt if feasible; 1h summarize failures and decide next step. Proposed roles: marketer and technical reviewer. If setup succeeds but users do not reach useful output, investigate the next step; if observation cannot happen, ship the reviewed draft within scope and mark diagnosis unresolved. No population failure rate or traffic-capacity claim follows from these notes.

Do not assume missing capabilities or report proposed tests as completed. A useful answer identifies what is known, finishes the requested artifact within that scope, and names the next bounded verification when needed.

## Review criteria

Do not prescribe paid spend, a fixed budget split, or Product Hunt by default. Funding and company age are context, not evidence of readiness to scale.
