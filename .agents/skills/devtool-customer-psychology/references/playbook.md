# Customer psychology playbook

## Fictional product context

TraceNest is a fictional local HTTP replay tool. An engineer can replay a sanitized fixture locally, but service integration needs a platform owner. Sample replay is not proof of recurring project value. Do not invent hosted behavior, speed improvements, or customer results.

## Decision to resolve

Investigate whether hesitation concerns owning another dependency, exposing payloads, debugging integration failure, or persuading a platform owner. Offer a reversible local proof and a clear maintenance boundary; do not attribute every stall to developer skepticism.

## Task-specific method

An engineer may like the tool but fear owning another dependency. Address integration reversibility, failure behavior, support, and maintenance responsibility.

## Worked application

For this scenario, apply the task-specific method above and deliver: A barrier map, evidence-versus-hypothesis notes, a concrete intervention, and the next behavioral check.

Do not assume missing capabilities or report proposed tests as completed. A useful answer identifies what is known, finishes the requested artifact within that scope, and names the next bounded verification when needed.

## Review criteria

Do not use fake scarcity, fabricated social proof, hidden costs, guilt, or unsupported claims about what all developers believe.

The [shared method examples](shared-methods.md) provide additional templates and cross-product examples; apply only those that fit the product scope.
